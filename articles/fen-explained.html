FEN: a chess position in one line

FEN (Forsyth–Edwards Notation) is the standard way to write down any position. Every chess program and site understands it.

Example

Starting position: rnbqkbnr/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/RNBQKBNR w KQkq - 0 1

The parts of the string

  1. Placement. Ranks 8 to 1 separated by “/”. Capitals are White pieces, lower case are Black: K king, Q queen, R rook, B bishop, N knight, P pawn. A digit is a run of empty squares.
  2. Side to move: w for White, b for Black.
  3. Castling: K and Q mean White can castle short and long, k and q the same for Black. “-” means none.
  4. En passant: the square where an en passant capture is possible, or “-”.
  5. Half-move clock for the 50-move rule.
  6. Move number.

Where to get a FEN

On Lichess and Chess.com you will find it in the “Share” menu or in analysis mode. Copy it and press Ctrl+V on the analysis page — the position appears on the board.
