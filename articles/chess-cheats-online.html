Is there a cheat for online chess?

Short answer: programs that suggest moves exist, but every major chess site is built to catch them. Here is how detection works in general terms, what happens after a ban, and what to use instead.

How chess sites spot engine help

Chess.com and Lichess don't need to see your screen. They look at the games themselves:

  • Move accuracy. Your moves are compared with a strong engine. People make small inaccuracies all the time; long runs of “perfect” moves stand out.
  • Sudden jumps in strength. A player who usually plays at one level and suddenly plays like a grandmaster gets reviewed.
  • Time per move. People think longer in hard positions and play obvious moves fast. With an engine, the time per move is often almost the same.
  • Reports and manual review. Opponents can report a game, and fair-play specialists check suspicious accounts.

The more games someone plays with help, the clearer the pattern becomes, so cheaters are caught sooner or later.

What happens to cheaters

  • The account is closed or marked for breaking fair-play rules.
  • Rating points won unfairly are usually returned to the opponents.
  • A new account created to get around the ban is closed too.

When using an engine is allowed

An engine is a normal training tool. Use it freely:

  • after the game, to find your mistakes — in game review;
  • on an analysis board — in position analysis;
  • against the computer, where hints are allowed;
  • for puzzles, openings and endgames.

The rule is simple: no outside help while a game against a real person is in progress.

How to get stronger quickly — the honest way

  1. Review every lost game: find the first move after which the evaluation dropped.
  2. Solve 10 puzzles a day: forks, pins, mates.
  3. Learn the common opening traps so they don't work against you.
  4. Before every move, ask yourself: what is my opponent threatening?
